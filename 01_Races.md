## SCHEMA NOTES
- PRINCIPLE: completeness over brevity. NO lore is cut — prose is preserved in full, only reformatted into clean, parsable sections.
- DATA LANGUAGE = English (single source of truth). The assistant renders flavor into Italian at OUTPUT.
- MEASUREMENTS = metric, pre-converted at source. Decimal POINT in data (1.5 m); render with comma at output (1,5 m).

# AU RA
> "We Xaela are nomads who roam and live off the land. My tribe was no different. Yet though we did no harm to those whose lands we traversed, they treated us as vermin. In their ignorance, they feared us, and that fear manifested as spite and violence. My loved ones and I were cursed at, spat upon, beaten, and worse. It was out of the desire to protect them that I began my research."
> — Alaqa the Witch

**Overview.** The Au Ra are a misunderstood people due to their physical appearance. Their scaled bodies and horns led many to fear them, believing them to be descendants of dragons or demons. This fear led to hate, which gave way to violence, leaving these proud people fewer in number across much of the world. These conditions have led the Au Ra to value their families beyond all else.

**Variable Size (physical).** Au Ra are characterized by their curved horns and intricate patterning of scales across their bodies. Sleek and powerful tails extend out from behind them. There is a striking difference in height between males and females: men range from just over 1.85 m to ~2.3 m, while women rarely exceed ~1.5 m. Their faces have sharp features and piercing eyes.

**Divided By Blood.** The Auri creation myth tells of two beings, the Dawn Father and the Dusk Mother. The dark-scaled Xaela are nomads who believe the blood of the Dusk Mother courses through their veins. On the Azim Steppe their traditional garb varies widely from tribe to tribe, each group adorning themselves in ways meaningful to their group. Outside tribal roots, Auri tend to blend into the fashion of the region they live in. The Xaela see themselves as free spirits who roam the lands hunting, gathering, and battling other tribes in honour of their ancestors. The light-scaled Raen instead assimilated into Eastern society; though a minority, their loyalty and service have been a great boon to the culture they ingrained themselves in.

**Peaks, Valleys and Plains.** The Auri homeland is Othard, a region to the far east. Most of it is covered by the sprawling Azim Steppe, across which the Xaela clans travel in close-knit tribes. The northern and western reaches of Othard are shielded by high mountain peaks and deep mist-shrouded valleys — it is from these mists that the Raen originally hailed. Today most Raen live in civilized society or hidden villages, leading peaceful lives.

**For My Family.** An Au Ra may set off on adventure for many reasons: a thirst for adventure, defense of their tribe, or a mission for a master. This could extend to searching for lost family members or seeking revenge for a slight against their family. Consideration for family helped define the two bloodlines: the Xaela kept their traditional ways and use power to protect their families, while the Raen felt a peaceful livelihood among the other races was the best way to protect their community.

**Au Ra Names.** The Au Ra approach names in two strikingly different ways. The Xaela share many names across tribes, but with as many spellings as there are tribes; they take their tribe's name as their surname. The Raen name themselves in their tongue so the name carries meaning. Only the ruling and warrior class legally hold surnames; commoners may take one, but it is not legally recognized. Raen in power often take surnames related to feats in battle, while commoners take names tied to their profession. Those from outside Hingan culture use names common to their home region.
- Male Raen: Tosetu, Keimei, Rakuyo, Hien, Genbu, Fugetsu, Unzan, Masatsuchi, Izuna, Sagan, Karaku, Chikamasa Rokuyari, Tanehiro, Yoshikore, Hidetake, Norishig.
- Female Raen: Yachiyo, Kikyo, Awayuki, Narumi, Azami, Kurenai, Tsubaki, Chidori, Suzume, Mizuki, Ibuki.
- Male Xaela: Arslang, Agasiletai, Chaghagan, Chuacenur, Bayan, Luvsan, Bujeg, Khalja, Charakha, Khaishan, Chigu, Jebke, Cirina, Narangerel, Altan.
- Female Xaela: Boragchin, Narengawa, Chotan, Qoyar, Khorchi, Ogul, Checheyigen, Altun, Eji, Baguchi, Odchigin, Khorghosun, Yesun, Ariunbold, Bayar.
- Clan Names: Adarkim, Bolir, Dalamiq, Khatayin, Oronir, Malqir, Ura, Goro, Oroq.
- Family Names: Yuzuka, Obinata, Yatsurugi, Rokuyari, Godo, Tabito, Yumishi, Musa.

**Au Ra Traits.**
- Ability Score Increase: WIS +2.
- Age: reach physical maturity ~16; live to ~80 years.
- Alignment: as a whole tend to neither good nor evil, law nor chaos. Xaela lean strongly chaotic (nomads outside city law); Raen enjoy the order of law. Often a product of their upbringing.
- Size: Medium (males ~2–2.1 m lean build; females rarely over ~1.5 m).
- Speed: 9 m walking.
- Cranial Horns: proficiency in the Perception skill.
- Scaled Bodies: while unarmored, AC = 11 + DEX modifier.
- Languages: Common and Auri. Auri has no numbered nouns or verbs, so an Au Ra sometimes mislabels things when speaking another language.
- Subrace: choose Xaela or Raen.

**Subrace — Raen.** The Raen live in harmony with the other civilized races of the East, fully assimilated into Doman culture and valued for their loyalty.
- Ability Score Increase: CHA +1.
- Polite Society: proficiency in the Persuasion skill.
- Unwavering Loyalty: advantage on saves against being charmed.

**Subrace — Xaela.** Hardened by nomadic life of hunting and warring, the Xaela have martial prowess and strength, and are more outgoing than the Raen. Their tribes are small but numerous, battling for Azim Steppe territory; they do not seek war, but use it to settle differences.
- Ability Score Increase: STR +1.
- Nomadic Instincts: proficiency in the Survival skill.
- Savage Attacks: on a melee weapon critical hit, roll one of the weapon's damage dice one additional time and add it to the crit's extra damage.
- Xaela Tribes: an extended list of the 51 Xaela tribes is in Appendix F (see Appendix C — Naming Info (below)).

**A Valuable Ally.** Au Ra are steadfast allies once befriended; once an Au Ra calls you friend, you are family.
> Hyur: "Every Hyur is different in their own way. They're an ambitious people who work hard for their future. We can live alongside them easily."
> Miqo'te: "Miqo'te don't differ much from us. We both have an appreciation for what the natural world can provide. I'm sure our tribes would clash if they crossed paths in Othard though."

# ELEZEN
> "For centuries, our nation has been punished for the sins of our forefathers. If our punishment is to end, I believe we must right the wrongs of antiquity and move forward as a nation united. Needless to say, my father did not share this opinion."
> — Ser Aymeric de Borel, Lord Commander of the Holy See

**Overview.** The Elezen are a race divided within themselves, their often arrogant attitudes keeping other races at arm's reach. The Wildwood live off the lush forests, using keen eyes to hunt and gather. The Duskwights are notorious for thievery and pillaging, victims of prejudice within the Twelveswood. The Ishgardians did away with the forest and founded an oligarchic city-state in northern Eorzea.

**Elegance and Pride.** Long legs, slender bodies, pointed ears and sharp features mark an Elezen, rivaled only by the poise with which they carry themselves. Their tall, thin frames differ within the race only by skin colour.

**Difference of Opinion.** Pride divides the race. Wildwood and Duskwights could not coexist and distanced themselves. The Wildwood believe they are the true rulers of the forests they hold dominion over. The Duskwights, who historically lived in cavern systems, have rejoined the surface but are eyed with prejudice by their Wildwood brethren. The Ishgardians founded a city-state in the North over a millennium ago with a wholly unique culture, closed off from the world.

**The Woods and the North.** The Elezen forest is the Black Shroud, brought to order by the Wildwood, who protect it from poachers and threats; Duskwights disregard the need to protect it and hunt freely. To the north, the city-state of Ishgard hides towering spires, homes and a grand cathedral behind giant gates. Living conditions are rough — the poor go hungry in the lower city while the wealthy live in excess, ruled by the religious teachings of the Holy See.

**Honour and Vengeance.** An Elezen may adventure for many reasons: an Ishgardian hungry for revenge against dragons may seek greater strength; a Wildwood may defend the Black Shroud from an encroaching threat; a Duskwight may seek honour and riches to support family or to prove they are more than a thief. Perhaps you simply seek glory in battle.

**Elezen Names.** Wildwood and Duskwight clans spell their names very similarly. Men's names are longer, always ending in -loix, -aux, -mont, or -geant. Women's names always end in -ne, -ette, -elle, or -ie. Despite similar first names, the tribes claimed surnames for each side; these never cross clans, and only those within the race know the difference.
- Male: Adeux, Beichault, Dreauchant, Eauplaux, Etrent, Gloffaux, Jodeaux, Joyent, Ulmant, Ulwex.
- Female: Adreanne, Iflione, Eaupline, Eranne, Etrene, Moriene, Peronne, Cummone, Phivonne, Paxeonne.
- Surnames: Breaunojuste, Douvionne, Feajorins, Gehon, Glauvefand, Grurubont, Nahame, Prihaux, Shogamie, Zuhepane.

**Elezen Traits.**
- Ability Score Increase: INT +2.
- Age: reach maturity ~18; live into their nineties.
- Alignment: Wildwood lean lawful (raised to protect the forest or within Ishgard's religious walls); Duskwights embrace the wild life and lean chaotic, viewing laws as arbitrary.
- Size: Medium (men up to ~2 m, rarely under ~1.9 m; women up to ~2 m, rarely under ~1.8 m).
- Superb Hearing: advantage on Perception checks made using hearing.
- Speed: 9 m walking.
- Languages: Common and one extra language of your choice.
- Subrace: choose Wildwood, Duskwight, or Ishgardian.

**Subrace — Wildwood.** Protectors of the forest, masters of woodcraft who defend their homes aggressively and live off the land.
- Ability Score Increase: DEX +1.
- Hawk Sight: the normal range of your ranged weapons increases by 6 m (cannot exceed the weapon's maximum range).
- Natural Shrewdness: proficiency in the Insight skill.

**Subrace — Duskwight.** Descendants of cave-dwellers of the Black Shroud (Gelmorra); light hair and dark skin set them apart. Natural cave explorers who built a reputation of banditry after struggling to fit into Gridanian society.
- Ability Score Increase: CON +1.
- Cave Dweller: advantage on Stealth checks in caves or in dim/dark lighting.
- Darkvision: see in dim light within 18 m as if bright, and in darkness as if dim (shades of grey only).

**Subrace — Ishgardian.** Left the Twelveswood over a millennium ago to found Ishgard; embroiled in a thousand-year war with dragons, balancing war and strict law.
- Ability Score Increase: STR +1.
- Conscription: proficiency with shortswords, shortbows, and spears.
- Halone's Scriptures: proficiency in the Religion skill.

**Arrogance or Confidence?** Elezen strongly believe in their abilities and birthrights. Many see this as arrogance, but past the haughty attitude lies a steadfast, reliable ally.

# GARLEAN
> "Only a man of power can rightly steer the course of civilization. And in this land of creeping mendacity, that one truth will prove its salvation."
> — Gaius van Baelsar, Legatus of the XIV Legion

**Overview.** The Garleans, who share the name of their Empire, suffered greatly before finding the means to fight back, becoming conquerors in their own right. They value advancement and ability, looking down on those content with stagnation — a dogged dedication that has brought them great success.

**Physical Description.** A hearty people whose life under imperial rule gave them strong bodies and minds. Their most striking feature is the pearl-like third eye in the center of the forehead, granting improved spatial recognition. Their great assets were met with one damning disadvantage: extreme difficulty manipulating magic — though some Garleans overcome even this to master aetherial manipulation.

**Displaced Conquerors.** Once living in the warm southern reaches of Ilsabard, the Garleans were forced north over time, crossing treacherous peaks to settle lands of long bitter winters and short growing seasons.

**Advent of Magitek.** Unable to manipulate aether, Garleans were defeated repeatedly by magic-wielders — until the discovery of ceruleum. Experimentation and engineering birthed magitek: machines and devices that mimic magical effects, swinging the pendulum in their favour. Within 50 years they formed a vast empire spanning three quarters of the great continents of Aldenard.

**Why They Adventure.** Two reasons stand out: to benefit the Empire as an ambitious patriot bettering Garlemald's position, or to seek freedom from a rigid hierarchy where advancement comes only through military activity — chasing dreams of freedom and peace away from home.

**Garlean Names.** Garlean names often declare one's title within the Empire as a middle name. Traditional names root in Latin, with a first and last name.
- Male: Aulus, Cid, Faust, Gaius, Ignatius, Kaius, Maxima, Nael, Nero, Noah, Quentin, Regula, Solus, Vitus, Zenos.
- Female: Adora, Alma, Belva, Cella, Elysia, Gloria, Julia, Livia, Lucia, Minerva, Octavia, Rheya, Silvia, Vesta.
- Middle Titles: van (Legatus), quo (Centurio), pyr (Assistant to Centurio), oen (veteran soldier), lux (head field engineer/medic), kir (veteran field engineer/medic), jen (field engineer/medic), dus (civil servant), cen (artisan), bas (merchant), aan (person from annexed lands).
- Surnames: Arvina, Asina, Baelsar, Brutus, Darnus, Gabranth, Galvus, Garlond, Hydrus, Junius, Lexentale, Messalla, Priscus, Scaeva.
- (A full list of Garlean Empire positions is in Appendix F / Appendix C — Naming Info (below).)

**Garlean Traits.**
- Ability Score Increase: STR +2, INT +1.
- Age: live as long as other races; maturity ~18; average lifespan ~80.
- Alignment: tend lawful, rooted in a highly ordered Empire where title and rank are paramount.
- Size: Medium (males ~1.8–2.1 m; females ~1.7–1.8 m).
- Imperial Education: proficiency in one skill of your choice and one set of artisan's tools of your choice.
- Spatial Insight: proficiency in the Investigation skill.
- Third Eye: proficiency in the Perception skill; the normal range of your ranged weapon attacks increases by 3 m.
- Speed: 9 m walking.
- Languages: Common, Garlean, and one extra language of your choice.

**Optional Racial Trait — Magically Inert.** You have great difficulty casting magic on your own power and require the aid of technology. In FFXIV the Garleans struggle to manipulate aether; it is not impossible (half-Garleans have no trouble). A spellcasting Garlean should flavor their focus as technology — e.g. a Garlean Wizard with a staff that recharges magic energy over time, regaining spell slots when the staff recharges on a long rest.

**Conquerors.** The Garlean Empire has earned the disdain of many. Most non-Garleans hold a hatred for them until their intentions are proven good.

# HROTHGAR
> "During the Third Astral Era, Queen Gunnhildr united the fragmented tribes of Hrothgar to rise up and drove off the Allagans, eventually going on to found the nation of Bozja."
> — Misija

**Overview.** Hrothgar had a difficult start, lacking the common tongue and often at odds with more well-spoken races. In time they learned the language and brokered peace, forming a friendly relationship between their home nation of Bozja and the rest of the world.

**Claws, Fur and Fury.** A powerful people with broad frames, feline reflexes and fierce appearances marked by sharp claws and pointed fangs. They suffer a large disproportion between males and females, with males a staggering majority. This led to a matriarchy in their homeland — which rallied many and drove others away, who became known as The Lost.

**A Question of Servitude.** The race split over conflicting ways of life. The Helions, identified by warm fur colours, are found mainly in the south and revolve their lives around their Queen — most are devout servants. The Lost lead a free-spirited, nomadic lifestyle, unbound by a Queen's whims, practicing various seasonal professions; their cool-hued fur reflects independence and the choice to leave the Queen's warm embrace.

**War Torn Homeland.** The Hrothgar hail from Bozja, a tropical region of southern Ilsabard. Born of an intense struggle against the Allagan empire centuries ago, Bozja is led by Queen Gunnhildr — a title bestowed on the woman with the greatest aptitude for a rare power of future sight, who selects her successor. After a severe accident leveled the capital, Bozja became a war-torn land controlled by the Garlean Empire, fought over by a resistance of patriots.

**It's My Life.** The ideological split between servitude and freedom drives them forward: Helions adventure in the name of their Queen to protect her or their homeland, while for The Lost, adventure is a key part of existence, needing to constantly travel to survive.

**Hrothgar Names.** Names are generally shared between Helions and The Lost, differing by traditional family names. Helion clans always have three-syllable names beginning with A; The Lost always end their name in "-asch."
- Male: Borimir, Deyan, Grugur, Mikac, Ludevit, Novak, Semir, Sudimir, Svante, Taman.
- Female: Anja, Gunnhildr, Hana, Jana, Katarina, Maja, Milena, Petra, Teodora.
- Helion Family Names: Amilan, Aredna, Arodica, Asvana, Astana.
- The Lost Family Names: Irryasch, Mildasch, Ovasch, Petyasch, Piryasch, Zelasch.

**Hrothgar Traits.**
- Ability Score Increase: STR +2.
- Age: live about as long as neighbouring races; maturity ~18; live into their nineties.
- Alignment: Helions lean lawful (raised to obey the Queen); The Lost lean chaotic (life on the road). Exceptions exist on both sides.
- Size: Medium. Tall, powerful frames marked by muscle and fur — at tallest ~2.1 m, low average ~1.9 m, generally over ~118 kg.
- Powerful Build: count as one size larger for carrying capacity and the weight you can push, drag, or lift.
- Claws: natural weapon for unarmed strikes; on a hit deals slashing damage equal to 1d6 + STR modifier (instead of the normal bludgeoning).
- Keen Smell: advantage on Perception checks that rely on smell.
- Speed: 9 m walking.
- Languages: Common and one extra language of your choice.
- Subrace: choose Helion or The Lost.

**Subrace — Helion.** From a matriarchal society where males greatly outnumber females; loyal and dutiful, they risk life and limb for their Queen and bring that ferocious dedication to defending friends. Warm fur colour.
- Ability Score Increase: DEX +1.
- Lionhearted: advantage on saves against being frightened.
- Aggressive: as a bonus action, move up to your speed toward an enemy you can see or hear; you must end this move closer to the enemy than you started.

**Subrace — The Lost.** Scattered and worldly, hailing from a bloodline that left the matriarchy. Often traders and mercenaries who prefer nomadic jobs. Cool fur colour reflects the lost warmth of their Queen.
- Ability Score Increase: CON +1.
- Contractor's Life: proficiency in the Persuasion skill.
- Unbending: advantage on saves against being charmed.

**Newly Formed Bonds.** The Hrothgar were initially at odds with other races; things smoothed over once they mastered the common tongue.
> Au Ra: "We see a lot of ourselves in the Au Ra, both Helions and The Lost alike. We can appreciate the path they walk."
> Roegadyn: "They're powerful and boisterous, not unlike ourselves. They could be great friends or great rivals depending on the day."
> Viera: "They too seem to experience issues within their kind when it comes to their opposite sex. Seems strange that usually the females only ever show themselves."

# HYUR
> "Thal's Balls…I'd forgotten what it was like to feel so alive! Not since leaving the Bloodsands have I had the privilege. Not since the Bull of Ala Mhigo hung up his swords… We shall have to do this again one day, when time allows."
> — Raubahn Aldynn, The Bull of Ala Mhigo

**Overview.** Around 1000 years ago the Hyur spread across the land in a great migration from the north, making homes wherever they could. Some took to the mountains, others to any city that would accept them. Today they are one of the most populous races in the world.

**Of Average Build.** A race of average-sized humanoids lacking distinguishing features such as pointed ears, immense size or horns. Over time they diverged into Midlanders — widespread, of all shapes and colours, prevalent in all cities — and Highlanders, taller and more powerfully built.

**A Matter of Location.** The difference was born from their homes. Highlanders are named for living at high elevation, especially mountains; tough conditions made them heartier and more powerful. Midlanders lived at sea level and across the world, with fewer extremes.

**People of the World.** Hyur make up a large percentage of the world's population. Over a thousand years ago they immigrated in massive waves, settling wherever their feet hit the ground. Highlanders are famed for the mountainous city of Ala Mhigo; in recent years Ala Mhigans were forced from their homes to migrate across the world, mixing into other cities.

**Glory and Prosperity.** A hardworking, ambitious people. For every Hyur there could be a different reason to adventure — the value of the journey itself, search for riches, on another's behalf (perhaps family at home), or to defend their people from a threat. It does not take much to bring a Hyur to action.

**Hyur Names.** In the common tongue. Midlanders take names appropriate to their culture; Highlanders take harsher-sounding names. As surnames, Midlanders take descriptive names for physical traits or profession; Highlanders earn surnames in battle, with martial or violent imagery.
- Male Midlander: Odard, Redwald, Kenward, Herebert, Acwuld, Edward, Roderic, Eadwine.
- Female Midlander: Rowena, Hilda, Agatha, Hrodwyn, Goldyna, Hounild, Edusa, Beyhild, Mathilda, Tonild, Somerhild, Rimilde.
- Male Highlander: Sigmundr, Arnar, Armond, Varin, Bard, Hordr, Ogmundr, Hallgrim, Thordr, Raubahn.
- Female Highlander: Marte, Cathrine, Yngvildr, Helene, Kolgrima, Katie, Myrun, Kaolin, Kaia, Kamilla.
- Midlander Surnames: Taylor, Faulkner, Browne, Parker, Forrest, River, Miller, Draper, Fletcher, Hill, Thatcher.
- Highlander Surnames: Fury, Burne, Savage, Stronge, Strongaxe, Blood, Furor, Shatterfist, Strongblade, Ironwill.

**Breadth of Bonds.** Hyur are prevalent throughout the world, so most welcome a Hyur without prejudice; they are generally met with courtesy wherever they wander.

**Hyur Traits.**
- Ability Score Increase: one ability score of your choice +1.
- Age: live ~75 years; sexual maturity ~16, full maturity ~20.
- Alignment: highly variable; tend to neither good nor evil, law nor chaos. They fall in line with the culture that raised them, but are generally good-hearted.
- Size: Medium (~1.5 m to ~2 m depending on heritage; Midlanders struggle to pass ~1.8 m, Highlanders easily pass it).
- Speed: 9 m walking.
- Spread of Culture: proficiency with a set of artisan's tools, gaming set, or musical instrument of your choice.
- Versatility: proficiency in two skills of your choice.
- Languages: Common and two other languages of your choice.
- Subrace: choose Midlander or Highlander (Padjal variant below).

**Subrace — Midlander.** A child of possibility, able to live almost anywhere without seeming out of place; highly adaptable and well educated.
- Ability Score Increase: INT +2.
- Variable Education: proficiency in an additional set of artisan's tools of your choice.

**Subrace — Highlander.** Warlike, seeing battle as the reason for life; no strangers to violence, hardened by mountain life into naturally powerful builds.
- Ability Score Increase: STR +2.
- Naturally Honed Body: proficiency in the Athletics skill.

**Variant — Padjal.** Rare children born to Hyur families of the Black Shroud, growing white animal horns and chosen by the elementals at birth, placing them in holy reverence in Gridania. They stop aging around their teens and have a natural latent power for conjury.
- Ability Score Increase: WIS +2.
- Nature's Blessing: proficiency in the Nature skill.

# LALAFELL
> "But I will see them suffer no longer… 'Tis mine intent that the ruling class of our golden city should take its place beside the common man in a fair and equitable republic. No more shall this nation bow to the whims of a privileged few."
> — Nanamo Ul Namo, Sultana of Ul'dah

**Overview.** Generally a cheery race. Long ago masters of agriculture, the Lalafell spread across the land along trade routes selling produce, becoming one of the most widespread races. The Dunesfolk eventually founded the desert city-state of Ul'dah. These tiny folk are highly ambitious and unafraid to seize opportunity.

**Tiny Yet Mighty.** Rarely growing to ~0.9 m tall. Their short, rotund bodies and feeble-looking legs deceive — Lalafell bodies are highly efficient, letting them travel long distances on short legs. Plainsfolk have larger, pointed ears that greatly improve hearing; Dunesfolk developed a film over the eyes protecting them from sun and sand. By tradition, Dunesfolk sometimes place gemstones tied to their zodiac sign on their foreheads.

**Products of the Land / Rolling Plains and Towering Dunes.** The Plainsfolk began as agriculturalists farming fertile lands across the southern seas, eventually trading their goods across the world. The Dunesfolk lacked farmable land and turned to animal husbandry, traditionally building homes on the backs of beasts of burden and traveling with their herds, before founding a great city of wealth where many Dunesfolk live today.

**Family and Fortune.** Lalafell care deeply for their small families and would do anything for them, adventuring to support or defend them. Many also carry great ambition: merchants travel everywhere, researchers seek knowledge in secret places, sellswords take jobs to prove themselves. Small, but they take on the world in a big way.

**Lalafell Names.** Both subraces name themselves using set rhyming schemes ruled by rhythm and repetition, combining syllables into playful sounds. They generally have no surnames unless royalty, which adds a family name in the middle. Each gender/subrace has its own rule set (each letter = a syllable or syllable pair):
- Naming convention — Male Plainsfolk: AB-CB | Female Plainsfolk: ABB-AB | Male Dunesfolk: AAB-CCB | Female Dunesfolk: AAB-AB.
- Male Plainsfolk: Budadi Yidadi, Egul Sagul, Erelo Forelo, Elehul Pedulehul, Gusis Shisis.
- Female Plainsfolk: Icucu Icu, Badada Bada, Usoso Uso, Kikuku Kiku.
- Male Dunesfolk: Wowokima Chichikima, Sheshehici Rorohici, Hihihebo Ruruhebo, Tutushuhu Kukushuhu, Gogoreba Susureba.
- Female Dunesfolk: Ejaja Eja, Babayi Bayi, Rerega Rega, Chochosha Chosha, Bobochu Bochu, Lelepu Lepu.

**Lalafell Traits.**
- Ability Score Increase: CHA +2.
- Age: reach maturity ~18; live into their mid-eighties.
- Alignment: generally good-natured (though some shrewd merchants stray); neither subrace inclines to law or chaos, individuals fall into all categories.
- Size: Small. Diminutive, no height difference by gender; ~0.8 m to just over ~0.9 m.
- Speed: 7.5 m walking.
- Cunning: advantage on all INT, WIS, and CHA saving throws against magic.
- Lalafellin Nimbleness: you can move through the space of any creature that is at least one size larger than you.
- Languages: Common and one other language.
- Subrace: choose Plainsfolk or Dunesfolk.

**Subrace — Plainsfolk.** Time spent in agriculture (or knowledge passed down) and highly sensitive ears set them apart.
- Ability Score Increase: DEX +1.
- Naturally Stealthy: you can attempt to hide even when obscured only by a creature at least one size larger than you.

**Subrace — Dunesfolk.** Adapted to harsh deserts; their homeland is known for riches and merchantry as much as corruption.
- Ability Score Increase: INT +1.
- Dustshield: your eyes have a protective film; advantage on saves against being blinded.
- Land of Merchants: proficiency in the Deception skill.

**Spoiler Subrace.** A third Lalafell subrace option is available in Appendix B (reflects minor FFXIV MSQ spoilers).

# MIQO'TE
> "The Nunh that I once fought also believed that his strrrength would save him. He was wrong, and I am now Nunh… Even when I was young I understood that victorrry goes to the patient, the cunning, the pragmatic."
> — U'odh Nunh, leader of the Drake Tribe

**Overview.** An aloof people who value independence. Centuries ago the Miqo'te migrated — Seekers spreading across the world in low numbers, Keepers settling dense forests. Traditionally they avoid other races, though over time more have integrated into society, with pockets of die-hard traditional tribes surviving.

**Lithe Hunters.** A feline race with large catlike ears, fanged teeth and long tails, a keen sense of smell and powerful legs, making them natural hunters. Seekers of the Sun have wider, thicker-furred ears and slit pupils to guard against sun exposure; Keepers of the Moon have longer, stylized ears with shorter fur and large, rounded pupils. Many more women are born into Miqo'te tribes and clans.

**Of Suns and Moons.** Two distinct ethnicities. Seekers live in male-dominant societies centered on an alpha male (Nunh) and worship the god Azeyma. Keepers live in a matriarchal society and worship the goddess Menphina.

**Arid Sands and Dense Woods.** The hot deserts of southern Thanalan host one of the most famed Seeker tribes; Seekers are found everywhere barring harsh-winter regions, and city-dwellers often discard tribal ties for urban norms. Keepers favour dense forests away from the sun's heat.

**Adventure Calls.** Approaches differ by ethnicity. Seekers are bold and quick to act but bore easily; their need for excitement may call them to explore, and traditional Seekers may leave to start their own clan as its alpha. # ROEGADYN

**Overview.** A physically powerful race with a thirst for life few can rival — loud, larger-than-life, fearless. Long ago the greatest pirates to sail the seas; adventure is in their blood.

**Size Matters.** Enormous, hulking, muscular humanoids; men and women alike tower over other races. They love contests of strength and courage, challenging one another. Fierce features and large flat noses; they favour durable, flexible clothing that allows easy movement.

**Maritime Reavers, Volcanic Hermits.** The Sea Wolves live up to their name through a storied ocean history; most communities are coastal, making their living from the sea through sailing, naval service, or mercenary work — most at home aboard a ship. The Hellsguard live inland around the volcanic mountains of Abalathia, guarding the material plane from undead spirits trying to return home.

**The Great Seas and Abalathia.** The Roegadyn hail from the northern seas — their name translates to "people of the rain." These seafarers live in coastal cities the world over as sailors, mercenaries and pirates. The Hellsguard live around the volcanoes of the Abalathia mountains, believing their home a sacred place tied to the afterlife; those who leave can be found in large cities near the mountains as sellswords or seekers of deeper magical knowledge.

**In Their Nature.** A boisterous race seeking challenges to test their power and bravery, rarely needing convincing to enter battle for a cause they believe in (though coin helps). Sea Wolves have a deep-seated thirst for adventure from their pirating history. Hellsguard grow up in solitude away from society and may set off down their mountain seeking a more engaging existence.

**Roegadyn Names.** Sea Wolves and Hellsguard follow very different conventions. # VIERA
> "The Viera may begin as part of the Wood, but it is not the only end that we may choose."
> — Fran the Sky Pirate

**Overview.** A beautiful, mysterious folk shrouded in mystery, most spending their lives in their woodland homes. Tall, slender bodies with rabbit-like traits give them an air of grace; long high-angled feet require high-heeled shoes, and dextrous clawed fingers complete their striking image.

**Separate Paths.** A race of both males and females who, outside necessary meetings to continue their race and the taking of grown male children, lead separate lives. Males live secretive, lonely lives shunning everyone, even kin, to pursue their sacred role as Wood-warders patrolling the Golmore Jungle and keeping outsiders away. The women are no less capable, turning their attention to defending their young and homes.

**People of the Wood.** "Viera" means "people of the wood," and true to their name they live in different forests of Dalmasca. Both Rava and Veena have tall, lithe frames and graceful rabbit traits. The Rava make their home in the Golmore Jungle and have umber skin and dark hair to blend in; the Veena have very light skin and fair hair to blend with the Skatay Range.

**Of Jungles and Forests.** Rava and Veena make homes exclusively in the Golmore Jungle and the primeval forests of the southwestern Skatay Range foothills, respectively — large sprawling canopies providing all the Viera need. The Skatay Range's white mountains serve the Veena as a secondary home. Both guard their homes with violence and without prejudice; intruders may meet a hostile reception.

**The Green Word.** The Viera's laws are the laws of the forest they live in. They take jobs under these laws and restrict contact with the outside world. Among their many self-imposed restrictions: if a Viera chooses to leave the forest, they are considered an outsider from that day forward. These strict laws often drive Viera with fire in their hearts away to seek adventure.

**Viera Names.** Often short, normally only 4 characters. The letter "J" is used as a vowel in their tongue, causing confusion for the unfamiliar. Despite close attention to bloodline purity, the Viera do not worry about lineage and have no family names.
- Male: Atan, Morn, Tjln, Frjn. (Note: true male examples are uncertain pending official material.)
- Female: Fran, Jote, Mjrn, Kryn, Ktjn.

**Viera Traits.**
- Ability Score Increase: DEX +2.

- Size: Medium. Rava are tall and lithe, averaging just over ~1.85 m; Veena are shorter, averaging ~1.7 m.
- Speed: 10.5 m walking.
- Lapine Hop: your maximum high jump and long jump distances are 3 m higher than normally calculated.
- Lucky: when you roll a 1 on an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, you can reroll the die and must use the new roll.
- Mask of the Wild: you can attempt to hide even when only lightly obscured by foliage, heavy rain, falling snow, mist, and other natural phenomena.
- Speak with Small Beasts: through sounds and gestures, you can communicate simple ideas with Small or smaller beasts.
- Languages: Common and Viera.
- Subrace: choose Rava or Veena.

**Subrace — Rava.** From the Golmore Jungle; a tall, powerful people with an intimidating presence, with unrelenting forest tactics.
- Ability Score Increase: WIS +1.
- Powerful Presence: proficiency in the Intimidation skill.

**Subrace — Veena.** From the forests of the Skatay Range; defend their home as vigorously as the Rava but, with more average size and softer appearance, have an easier time leaving home. Many left amid rising war near their homeland to join pockets of civilization.

## AMALJ'AA
> "I am the flame that quenches the shadow! The fist that crashes villainy! I am the warleader of the Brotherhood of Ash, Hamujj Gah, and my name shall be the last you utter with your dying screams!"
> — Hamujj Gah

**Imposing Lizards.** The Amalj'aa are hulking lizard-like humanoids who dwarf even Roegadyn in size and power. Naturally muscular and blessed with nigh-limitless stamina, they are accustomed to harsh arid regions and survive on minimal water. There is little physical difference between males and females.

**Rolling Golden Fields.** They hail from the fields of Paglth'an in northern Thanalan, travelling vast arid plains herding drakes and sheep and hunting local wildlife to survive.

**A Path Cleared By Flame.** They live in a hierarchy of seven distinct levels. When the young come of age they receive a second name placing them in a tier based on their individual talents. They live in several warbands presided over by a great war chief. When a child is born, the parents offer one of their herd for a feast and burn the bones; the ash coats the newborn's skin to represent the time Ifrit gifted his sacred flames to their people.

**Hierarchy (tiers).**
- War Chief tier: leaders at the head of the seven dominant Amalj'aa tribes, ruling the clans; succession primarily by bloodline.
- Commanders — Zoh (the "sky" tier): advisors who counsel the war chiefs and decide how the tribes are governed.
- Commanders — Koh (the "earth" tier): bureaucrats who carry out the mandates of the Zoh.
- Commanders — Teh (the "spring" tier): priests performing divination rites before battle and other rituals.
- Soldiers — Chah (the "summer" tier): warriors overseeing military operations, found on the frontlines of inter-tribal conflicts and war with other races.
- Soldiers — Roh (the "autumn" tier): enforcers who maintain peace and punish those who break tribal law.
- Soldiers — Bob (the "winter" tier): builders responsible for camp construction and tasks requiring engineering.

**One's Place in the Herd (names).** Amalj'aa names follow a syllable, syllable, double-consonant pattern; the final consonants are always double b's, d's, g's, j's, k's or v's. The name is completed at coming of age with the tier suffix.
- Names: Afajj Koh, Atabb Chah, Fibubb Gah, Hepugg Roh, Maladd Chah, Narujj Boh, Nayokk Roh, Nazagg Gah, Nezedd Gah, Pegujj Chah, Rodegg Chah, Tanadd Gah, Yadovv Gah.

**Amalj'aa Traits.**

## ANANTA
**Slithering Beauty.** The Ananta are a race of serpentine women whose upper bodies resemble Hyur women, but whose lower bodies are that of a serpent, used to propel themselves along the ground. They molt regularly to renew the sheen of their scales.

**Gemstones of the Mountains.** They call the mountains of Gyr Abania home, formerly living as one large tribe presided over by a broodmother who could trace her bloodline to the first Ananta. They have been known to rescue people in trouble in the mountains.

Ananta lay eggs, forming strong mother-daughter bonds, though the concept of family is loose — they tend to view the world as the self and everyone else.

**Naming Conventions.** The Ananta take names from their native tongue, often words associated with beauty.

## BANGAA

Though lizard-like in appearance they are actually mammalian — making such a comparison directly is a grave insult. Casually they are called "long-ears." Tall and muscular, they excel at physical tasks both professionally (stone masons) and martially (warriors and guards).

**A Land Beneath a Blazing Sun.** Long integrated into Dalmascan society, the Bangaa make up about twenty percent of its population. Though long-lived, they bear young infrequently, keeping their numbers balanced. Their stone masonry was a key contribution to Dalmasca's impressive buildings.

**Cultural Beliefs.** Before joining Dalmascan society the Bangaa were a collection of clans differentiated by skin hue, led by yellow-brown hued Bangaa who acted as religious leaders. After intermingling in the cities, hue became an unreliable lineage marker. Today they serve as soldiers, guards, labourers, and (for the exceptionally skilled) royal knights of Rabanastre.

## EORZEAN KOBOLD
**A Life Beneath The Land.** Kobolds are a mole-like people who dwell in a vast tunnel system in Vylbrand, beneath Mount O'Ghomoro. They stand hunched but are bipedal, often clad in metal armor; beneath their helmets are beady red eyes and rounded teeth.

**Homeland.** The region around Mount O'Ghomoro is rich with precious gemstones and ores. Surrounded by thick forests, ancient ruins, and hot springs, it offers the kobolds countless places to excavate.

**A Never Ending Labour.** They view gems and minerals as gifts from their father, Titan, treating such materials with religious vigor. Pious and industrious, they are renowned metalworkers and alchemists, even cultivating Bomb voidsent for mining. Their society is a meritocracy organized into Digs (from Dig 1 down to Dig 789); the higher one's dig, the more skilled and industrious. Kobolds move up and down the hierarchy as they improve.

**Naming Convention.** Kobold names are extremely short single-syllable sounds, with the clan name preceding the first name (e.g. "Zu Ga" = "Ga" of the "Zu" clan). Identical names are common and rendered unimportant by their fixation on rank and occupation (e.g. "3rd Order Patriarch Zu Ga").
- Names: Be Gu, Bi Bi, Ga Zi, Ge Bu, Go Zu, Ze Bu.

**Kobold Traits.**
- Ability Score Increase: WIS +2, STR +1.

- Alignment: living in a rigid hierarchy, tend lawful.
- Size: Medium.
- Speed: 9 m walking.
- Darkvision: see in dim light within 18 m as if bright, and in darkness as if dim (shades of grey only).

- Kobold Outfitting: proficiency in light and medium armor.
- Superior Darkvision: your darkvision has a radius of 36 m.
- Stonecunning: on an Intelligence (History) check related to the origin of stonework, you are considered proficient and add double your proficiency bonus.
- Tool Proficiency: proficiency with a set of artisan's tools of your choice.

## GNATH
> "Oh, my mind, it is full of questions! Heavy questions. I fear many things. But I fear losing myself the most."

**Physical Description.** The Gnath appear as upright antlings with large sloping heads, sharp mandibles and hunched, carapace-covered backs. They often wear cloaks to hide their segmented, gaunt bodies and upper limbs.

## IXAL
> "For war with forest people, or pleasing greedy goddess, we care not! Fixed on the heavens, our eyes are!"
> — Sezul Totoloc

**Fearsome and Nigh Featherless.** The Ixal are tall, bird-faced humanoids with long clawed limbs and tough skin. Long ago they had feathered bodies capable of flight, but over generations in Xelphatol their feathers reduced to tufts on their elbows.

**The Mountain Of Harsh Winds.** They call the mountainous Xelphatol home — poor soil, treacherous terrain and violent winds. Through inventiveness and determination they eke out an existence, frequently raiding the Black Shroud for resources.

**Greed from Need.** Their true origin places them as chimerical creations (Iksalion) that served the Allagans' Garuda Airborne Division. They maintain a militaristic chain of command, the blood of former commanders flowing through today's leaders. ## Xaela Tribes of the Azim Steppe (51)
A Xaela's surname is the name of their tribe. ~51 known tribes wander the Steppe; new tribes may form or vanish.
1. Adarkim — the largest Xaela tribe. Not the most skilled at battle, but overwhelm with numbers, taking losses in stride knowing future victories over weaker tribes will replenish their ranks.
2. Angura — a small tribe keeping to the mountainous northeastern Othard; glacier glare has turned their skin a deep rusty tone.
3. Arulaq — thought lost 200 years ago, only recently rediscovered living in a secluded northern valley.
4. Avagnar — defeated and absorbed by the Adarkim; several proud members still secretly use the ancestral name, which could mean death if discovered.
5. Bairon — mid-sized tribe of the southern deserts; masters of survival, trained from a young age to drink their own bodily fluids to venture into the driest places.
6. Bayaqud — of the western edges; women traditionally take several husbands, as did the founding matron 2000 years ago.
7. Bolir — a small tribe that collects beastkin dung, dries it into charcoal in temporary kilns, and sells it to other tribes.
8. Borlaaq — a tribe of all women; breeding with outside men is allowed, but a male born into the tribe is given up within a year.
9. Buduga — an all-male tribe that increases its ranks only through battle and kidnapping.
10. Dalamiq — abandoned the nomadic life, building a small village on an islet in the great inner river; once worshipped the now-fallen lesser moon.
11. Iriq — follows the Borlaaq, taking in male children given up by the female warriors and raising them as their own.
12. Jhungid — the second largest tribe; mortal enemies of the Kharlu; subjugate smaller tribes to swell their ranks for an annual battle for eastern coastlands.
13. Kharlu — the third largest tribe; mortal enemies of the Jhungid; the same annual battle for the eastern coastlands.
14. Khatayin — largely unseen; hunt goats in the mountains nine moons of the year, spending three at the foot of the north range on dried meat.
15. Malqir — a western tribe whose leader is chosen by Kharaqiq (a chess-like game on a circular three-ringed board) rather than brawn.
16. Mankhad — fight with blow-darts of bone dipped in pufferfish poison; can disable a target from 200 paces.
17. Mierqid — a desert tribe that travels between over a hundred secret buried supply caches, restocked each annual visit.
18. Noykin — master trainers of wild steppe horses; their horsewives can break any beast within a week.
19. Olkund — selective breeding has made central-steppe males average over two and a half yalms tall; females remain average height.
20. Dazkar — males handle household duties and rarely leave the yurts; females hunt and are famed as some of the most accurate archers in the realm.
21. Oronir — all members believe themselves direct descendants of Azim, the sun god.
22. Oroq — create sleds of reeds dipped in horse fat to move possessions and young children about the grasslands.
23. Qerel — warriors wear full suits of armor crafted from the bones of steppe tigers they kill by their own hands at their coming of age.
24. Torgud — a desert tribe that wears no clothes, covering their bodies in white paint (mud, lime, and bone meal) to reflect the sun.
25. Tumet — children, at their tenth summer, are tied to a sacred tree as the tribe moves on; those who free themselves and reach the next location are named and admitted.
26. Ugund — the dead have their heads placed in a jar of fermented goat milk; once the liquid evaporates (is "drunk"), the head is buried under an anthill so the ants carry the spirit to the afterlife.
27. Uyagir — gave up the nomadic life; reside in limestone caves on the northern edge of the southern deserts, believed dug by giant oliphant-like beetles.
28. Dhoro — an elusive tribe that avoids contact; lookouts flee the moment an outsider is spotted.
29. Orben — ride boats woven from reeds and reinforced with scales from their own skin, up and down the great inner river.
30. Ejinn — a river tribe that swims from place to place; members can hold their breath up to a quarter bell, often migrating submerged to avoid hostile tribes.
31. Dotharl — an extremely violent tribe that revels in massacre and is taught from a young age not to fear death; high mortality keeps their numbers from growing.
32. Hotgo — recently massacred by the Dotharl; the only survivors were those travelling alone; known for vibrant face paints that change with mood.
33. Sagahl — see all beastkin as equals with man and refuse to eat or use them; their diet is mainly steppe shrubs and vilekin.
34. Kahkol — made up of orphans and refugees from destroyed tribes; many combine their old tribe's name with Kahkol.
35. Kha — live on the fringes of Xaela lands, actively seeking contact with non-Auri peoples and adopting aspects of their cultures.
36. Mol — devout worshippers of the elder gods who consult their deities (via a shaman conduit) before any tribe decision.
37. Gesi — masters of the slingspear, a mammoth-bone javelin flung with a leather sling for greater range and power.
38. Kagon — a nocturnal desert tribe worshipping Nhaama (moon goddess); they shun sunlight (Azim's evil), giving them uncharacteristically pale skin.
39. Goro — believe horses to be perfect beings; each member at coming of age is married to a horse of the opposite sex, with reproductive mates chosen by lots.
40. Gharl — fill a sacred urn with soil of each camp and dump it at the next, a tradition so old people believe most of the steppe is now of one soil.
41. Dataq — cover possibly the most area in their migrations, rarely stopping longer than a few bells; they sleep in the saddle.
42. Haragin — a coastal tribe whose legends tell of ancestors who sailed the eastern ocean and returned with tales of grey monoliths and fire-breathing steel demons.
43. Ura — a mountain-dwelling tribe that mines precious ores and trades them with steppe tribes for food.
44. Moks — an "invisible" tribe whose members are spread (unknowingly to those tribes) across many others; they communicate via hidden hand signals when tribes meet.
45. Geneq — employ a complex system of whistles and clicks resembling cloud- and wavekin, alongside the common Xaela tongue.
46. Horo — drink copious water to bloat their bellies, since appearing overweight signals affluence and power.
47. Himaa — one in three pregnancies yields twins, so over half the tribe has a doppelganger — an advantage that confuses enemies into believing the dead have risen.
48. Malaguld — one of the few tribes that accepts exiled or persecuted Raen into their circle.
49. Urumet — a desert tribe that travels carrying its elders on their shoulders to see farther across the flat desert.
50. Qalli — the "songbirds of the steppe," who communicate through song, attaching a melody to their words.
51. Qestir — refuse to speak, believing all words are lies and that a man's actions are the purest form of communication.

# CHAPTER 4 — ROLE ACTION FEATS
Widely-shared battle techniques representing the role actions of FFXIV. For use alongside the 5th Edition Player's Handbook.

## Role of the Vanguard
- Increase your Strength or Constitution score by 1, to a max of 20.
- Provoke: as a bonus action, challenge a creature you can see within 9 m; it has disadvantage on attacks made against creatures other than you until the start of your next turn. Uses = proficiency bonus, recovered on a long rest.
- Rampart: as a reaction, reduce damage you receive by your proficiency bonus until the start of your next turn. Once per short rest.

## Heart of the Vanguard
*Prerequisite: Role of the Vanguard.*
- Increase your Strength or Constitution score by 1, to a max of 20.
- Interject: when a creature you can see casts a spell within 1.5 m of you, as a reaction make a weapon attack against it; on a hit it must make a concentration save (DC 8 + your STR or DEX modifier + proficiency bonus) or the spell fails. Damage applies regardless. Uses = proficiency bonus, recovered on a long rest.
- Reprisal: as a bonus action, emit a dominating aura in a 3 m radius; enemy creatures dealing damage in the area have it reduced by your proficiency bonus until the end of your next turn. Uses = proficiency bonus, recovered on a long rest.

## Role of the Soothe Sayer
- Increase your Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma score by 1, to a max of 20.
- Cleric Stance: when you cast a spell that recovers hit points you may instead change the healing into radiant damage, dealing bonus damage equal to your spellcasting modifier (the spell's healing must require a die roll). Uses = proficiency bonus, recovered on a long rest.
- Surecast: when hit by an attack, as a reaction you can auto-pass any concentration checks and saving throws against effects that forcibly move you until the start of your next turn. Once per long rest.

## Heart of the Soothe Sayer
*Prerequisite: Role of the Soothe Sayer.*
- Increase your Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma score by 1, to a max of 20.
- Rescue: as an action, a willing creature you can see within 18 m is instantly teleported to a space within 1.5 m of you. Once per long rest.
- Swiftcast: cast a spell with a casting time of 1 action as a bonus action instead. Once per long rest.

## Role of the Destroyer
- Increase your Strength or Dexterity score by 1, to a max of 20.
- Arm's Length: when targeted by a melee weapon attack, as a reaction the attack has disadvantage and the attacker can't make opportunity attacks against you until the end of your next turn. Uses = proficiency bonus, recovered on a long rest.
- Recuperate: as a bonus action, recover 2d4 + your character level hit points. Once per long rest.

## Heart of the Destroyer
*Prerequisite: Role of the Destroyer.*
- Increase your Strength or Dexterity score by 1, to a max of 20.
- Feint: when you hit a creature with a melee weapon attack, it must make a Constitution save (DC 8 + proficiency bonus + your STR or DEX modifier); on a failure any damage it deals is reduced by your proficiency bonus and it has disadvantage on attack rolls until the start of your next turn. Uses = proficiency bonus, recovered on a long rest.
- True North: as a bonus action, until the start of your next turn you make all melee weapon attack rolls with advantage. Once per long rest.

## Role of the Magus
- Increase your Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma score by 1, to a max of 20.
- Swiftcast: cast a spell with a casting time of 1 action as a bonus action instead. Once per long rest.
- Lucid Dreaming: when you cast a spell, double your proficiency bonus to your spell attack roll or spell save DC for that spell until the start of your next turn. Once per long rest.

## Heart of the Magus
*Prerequisite: Role of the Magus.*
- Increase your Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma score by 1, to a max of 20.
- Addle: as a bonus action, a creature you can see within 9 m must make a Wisdom save (DC 8 + proficiency bonus + your spellcasting modifier); on a failure any damage it deals is reduced by your proficiency bonus and it has disadvantage on saves against your spells until the start of your next turn. Uses = proficiency bonus, recovered on a long rest.
- Surecast: when hit by an attack, as a reaction you can auto-pass any concentration checks and saving throws against effects that forcibly move you until the start of your next turn. Once per long rest.

## Role of the Sniper
- Increase your Dexterity score by 1, to a max of 20.
- Arm's Length: when targeted by a melee weapon attack, as a reaction the attack has disadvantage and the attacker can't make opportunity attacks against you until the end of your next turn. Uses = proficiency bonus, recovered on a long rest.
- Peloton: as a bonus action, you and all allied creatures within 9 m gain +3 m base movement until the start of your next turn. Uses = proficiency bonus, recovered on a long rest.

## Heart of the Sniper
*Prerequisite: Role of the Sniper.*
- Increase your Dexterity score by 1, to a max of 20.
- Blunt Shot: when a creature you can see within 9 m begins to cast a spell, as a reaction make a ranged weapon attack against it; on a hit it must make a concentration save (DC 8 + your DEX modifier + proficiency bonus) or the spell fails. Damage applies regardless. Uses = proficiency bonus, recovered on a long rest.
- Maiming Shot: when you land a ranged weapon attack, the target must make a Constitution save (DC 8 + proficiency bonus + your DEX modifier) or its speed becomes 0 until the start of your next turn. Damage applies regardless. Uses = proficiency bonus, recovered on a long rest.
